# manas

English candidates: **mind**.

## Root and derivation

**Root checked:** Dhātupāṭha 4.73, `mana jñāne`: man in knowing. [Text](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_z_misc_major_works/dhatupatha.html). This checks the root entry, not a complete derivation of the noun. Dhātupāṭha 8.9 also has `manu avabodhane`. The inherited relationship with English mind and Latin mens remains a separate, unchecked comparative-linguistic claim.

## Textual evidence

### Caraka Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 8.4 (opening sentence)

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/carakasamhita.xml).

```text
atīndriyaṃ punarmanaḥ sattvasaṃjñakaḥ cetaḥ ityāhureke
```

Literal English: Mind, beyond the senses, is called sattva; some call it cetas. [The e-text’s grammatical form is retained.]

### Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.3.3 (GRETIL KaU 3.3)

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_kathopaniSad.htm).

```text
ātmānaṃ rathinaṃ viddhi śarīraṃ ratham eva tu /
buddhiṃ tu sārathiṃ viddhi manaḥ pragraham eva ca //
```

Literal English: Know the self as the chariot-owner, and the body as the chariot itself; know buddhi as the charioteer and the mind as the rein.

### Sāṃkhya Kārikā 27

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_IzvarakRSNa-sAMkhyakArikA.htm).

```text
ubhayātmakam atra manaḥ saṃkalpakam indriyaṃ ca sādharmyāt /
guṇapariṇāmaviśeṣān nānātvam bāhyabhedāc ca //
```

Literal English: Here the mind is of both natures, forming conceptions, and is a faculty through similarity of properties; diversity is from differences in transformations of the guṇas and from external difference.

## Reading of the proposed English

Mind is sound. The Kārikā says saṃkalpaka, conception-forming; it does not define manas simply as doubt. A verbal root does not make “the thinking” the best English noun.
