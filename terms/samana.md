# samāna

English candidates: **assimilating wind; digestive wind**.

## Root and derivation

The prefix/root analysis proposed for the vital winds has **not yet been checked against a grammatical source**. Functional translation is kept separate from derivation.

## Textual evidence

### Suśruta Saṃhitā, Nidānasthāna 1.16

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/susrutasamhita.xml).

```text
āmapakvāśayacaraḥ samāno vahnisaṅgataḥ/ so+annaṃ pacati tajjāṃśca viśeṣānvivinakti hi//
```

Literal English: Moving in the receptacles of unprocessed and processed food, samāna is joined with the digestive fire. It cooks food and discriminates the products arising from it.

### Caraka Saṃhitā, Cikitsāsthāna 28.8

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/carakasamhita.xml).

Literal English of the passage: Occupying the channels that carry sweat, doṣa and water, and situated beside the internal fire, samāna gives strength to the fire.

Comparison: Caraka emphasizes support of the digestive fire and a wider channel context; Suśruta explicitly names cooking food and distinguishing its products. These support digestive coordination, while neither passage states a general function of gathering information.

## Reading of the proposed English

“Gathering to the center” is not the function stated here. Assimilation or digestive coordination is better grounded than gathering questions and answers. SARIT represents elided initial vowels by `+`; that editorial notation is retained. Anusvāra glyph ṁ is normalized to ṃ.
