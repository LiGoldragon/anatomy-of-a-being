# vyāna

English candidates: **distributing wind; pervasive wind**.

## Root and derivation

The prefix/root analysis proposed for the vital winds has **not yet been checked against a grammatical source**. Functional translation is kept separate from derivation.

## Textual evidence

### Suśruta Saṃhitā, Nidānasthāna 1.17–18

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/susrutasamhita.xml).

```text
gulmāgnisādātīsāraprabhṛtīn kurute gadān/ kṛtsnadehacaro vyāno rasasaṃvahanodyataḥ//
svedāsṛksrāvaṇaścāpi pañcadhā ceṣṭayatyapi/ kruddhaśca kurute rogān prāyaśaḥ sarvadehagān//
```

Literal English: [Samāna disturbed] causes abdominal masses, sinking digestive fire, diarrhoea and similar diseases. Vyāna moves through the whole body, intent on conveying rasa; it also causes sweat and blood to flow and produces movement in five ways. When angry it generally causes diseases of the whole body.

## Reading of the proposed English

Distribution is a supported functional simplification. The seat is the whole body, not a single central organ. Verse 17 begins with the preceding wind’s disorders. SARIT represents elided initial vowels by `+`; that editorial notation is retained. Anusvāra glyph ṁ is normalized to ṃ.
