# prakṛti

English candidates: **nature; primordial material nature**.

## Root and derivation

**Root checked:** Dhātupāṭha 8.10, `ḍukṛñ karaṇe`: kṛ in doing/making. [Text](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_z_misc_major_works/dhatupatha.html). This checks the root entry, not a complete derivation of the noun. pra + kṛti is the proposed morphological analysis; a full suffix derivation remains unchecked.

## Textual evidence

### Sāṃkhya Kārikā 22

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_IzvarakRSNa-sAMkhyakArikA.htm).

```text
prakṛter mahāṃs tato 'haṃkāras tasmād gaṇaś ca ṣoḍaśakaḥ /
tasmād api ṣoḍaśakāt pañcabhyaḥ pañca bhūtāni //
```

Literal English: From prakṛti comes the great principle; from that I-making; from that the group of sixteen; and from five of those sixteen the five gross elements.

## Reading of the proposed English

Nature is conventional; “making-forth” is an etymological gloss. The root prakṛti is distinguished from its evolutes; calling it simply everything other than the witness blurs that technical distinction.
