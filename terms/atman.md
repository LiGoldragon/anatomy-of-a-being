# ātman

English candidates: **self**.

## Root and derivation

**Checked traditional alternatives:** Nirukta 3.15: `ātmā atater vā āpter vā api vā āpta iva syād yāvad vyāpti bhūta iti` (dots used as word dividers in the e-text are rendered as spaces). [Text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_yAska-nirukta.htm). Yāska offers atati and āpnoti-based explanations; he does not offer √an in this passage. The proposed historical connection with breath/√an and German Atem is **unchecked here**, not established by this nirvacana.

## Textual evidence

### Caraka Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 1.42

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/carakasamhita.xml).

```text
śarīrendriyasattvātmasaṃyogo dhāri jīvitam /
nityagaścānubandhaśca paryāyairāyurucyate //
```

Literal English: Life is called, by synonyms, the conjunction of body, faculties, mind and self; the sustaining, the living, the continuously going, and the continuing connection.

### Caraka Saṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 1.46–47

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/carakasamhita.xml).

```text
sattvamātmā śarīraṃ ca trayametattridaṇḍavat /
lokastiṣṭhati saṃyogāttatra sarvaṃ pratiṣṭhitam //
sa pumāṃścetanaṃ tacca taccādhikaraṇaṃ smṛtam /
vedasyāsya tadarthaṃ hi vedo 'ayaṃ saṃprakāśitaḥ //
```

Literal English: Mind, self and body: this triad is like a three-staff support. Through conjunction the world stands; in it everything is established. That is the person, that is conscious, and that is held to be the subject of this science; for its sake this science has been made known.

## Reading of the proposed English

Self is faithful. “Breath” is an etymological hypothesis, not a replacement translation for ātman in these verses. An identity with Greek psyche cannot be inferred from the checked Sanskrit.
