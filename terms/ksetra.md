# kṣetra

English candidates: **field**.

## Root and derivation

Proposed √kṣi, dwell/possess, plus an instrumental/local noun formation: **not yet checked against a grammatical text**. “Place dwelt in” is an etymological gloss, not the only lexical meaning.

## Textual evidence

### Bhagavad Gītā 13.1 (critical-text numbering)

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/ext/bhgce__u.htm).

```text
idaṃ śarīraṃ kaunteya kṣetram ity abhidhīyate /
etad yo vetti taṃ prāhuḥ kṣetrajña iti tadvidaḥ //
```

Literal English: This body, son of Kuntī, is called the field. Whoever knows it, those who know that call him the field-knower.

### Bhagavad Gītā 13.5–6 (critical-text numbering)

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/ext/bhgce__u.htm).

```text
mahābhūtāny ahaṃkāro buddhir avyaktam eva ca /
indriyāṇi daśaikaṃ ca pañca cendriyagocarāḥ //
icchā dveṣaḥ sukhaṃ duḥkhaṃ saṃghātaś cetanā dhṛtiḥ /
etat kṣetraṃ samāsena savikāram udāhṛtam //
```

Literal English: The great elements, I-making, buddhi and the unmanifest; the ten faculties and the one, and the five ranges of the faculties; desire, aversion, pleasure, pain, the aggregate, sentience and persistence: this is stated briefly as the field together with its modifications.

## Reading of the proposed English

Field is exact. In 13.5–6 the field includes mental functions. The list says “ten and one”; identifying the one as mind is the conventional reading, not an explicit occurrence of the word manas in that line. Editions adding Arjuna’s introductory question number these verses 13.2 and 13.6–7.
