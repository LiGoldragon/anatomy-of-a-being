# vāta; vāyu

English candidates: **wind; vital wind**.

## Root and derivation

The proposed root relation and derivation are **unchecked**.

## Textual evidence

### Suśruta Saṃhitā, Nidānasthāna 1.12

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/susrutasamhita.xml).

```text
prāṇodānau samānaśca vyānaścāpāna eva ca/ sthānasthā mārutāḥ pañca yāpayanti śarīriṇam//
```

Literal English: Prāṇa and udāna, samāna, vyāna and apāna: the five winds, each established in its seat, sustain the embodied being.

## Reading of the proposed English

“Movement” names a characteristic function, not a literal lexical equivalent. The five types differ by seat and function, not by direction alone. The passage does not establish that every bodily process is carried by a single force.

## Further grammatical check

**Checked root:** Dhātupāṭha 2.45, `vā gatigandhanayoḥ` ([text](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_z_misc_major_works/dhatupatha.html)), gives motion and gandhana. This checks the proposed wind/movement root connection, not the complete formation of either vāta or vāyu.
