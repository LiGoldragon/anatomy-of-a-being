# āma

English candidates: **raw; unripe; undigested material**.

## Root and derivation

Root/suffix derivation is **unchecked**. The broad lexical sense raw is not limited to digestive pathology.

## Textual evidence

### Caraka Saṃhitā, Vimānasthāna 2.9 (context, not a lexical definition)

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/carakasamhita.xml).

```text
mātrayā 'apyabhyavahṛtaṃ pathyaṃ cānnaṃ na jīryati/
cintāśokabhayakrodhaduḥkhaśayyāprajāgaraiḥ//
```

Literal English: Even wholesome food consumed in measure is not digested because of worry, grief, fear, anger, an uncomfortable bed and wakefulness.

## Reading of the proposed English

The checked surrounding discussion is about āmapradoṣa; this verse supplies context, not an explicit definition of āma. “Undigested material” is preferable to generic residue, which can also mean the products left after successful digestion. A defining passage and complete theory remain pending.

## An explicit definition in a supplementary medical source

### Aṣṭāṅgahṛdayasaṃhitā, Sūtrasthāna 13.25–26

Status: **checked in the Sanskrit e-text**. [SARIT text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/astangahrdayasamhita.xml). This is Vāgbhaṭa, supplementary to the three texts originally requested; it is not silently attributed to Caraka or Suśruta.

```text
ūṣmaṇo 'lpabalatvena dhātum ādyam apācitam /
duṣṭam āmāśayagataṃ rasam āmaṃ pracakṣate // 25 //
anye doṣebhya evātiduṣṭebhyo 'nyonyamūrchanāt /
kodravebhyo viṣasyeva vadanty āmasya sambhavam // 26 //
```

The source’s editorial compound hyphens have been removed; `evāti-duṣṭebhyo` is rendered `evātiduṣṭebhyo`.

Literal English, 25: The first constituent, rasa, not digested because of the small strength of heat, corrupted and situated in the receptacle of unprocessed food, they call āma.

Literal English, 26: Others say āma originates from the doṣas themselves when excessively corrupted, through mutual interaction, as poison from kodrava grains.

The following 13.27 calls the doṣas and affected constituents associated with it sāma. The alternative account in 13.26 prevents the oversimplification that āma has only one universally stated origin. Undigested or improperly processed material is better grounded than generic residue. A corresponding explicit lexical definition in the originally requested three-text set remains unverified.
