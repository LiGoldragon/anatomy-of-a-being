# ahaṃkāra

English candidates: **I-making; self-appropriation**.

## Root and derivation

**Root checked:** Dhātupāṭha 8.10, `ḍukṛñ karaṇe`: kṛ in doing/making. [Text](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_z_misc_major_works/dhatupatha.html). This checks the root entry, not a complete derivation of the noun. aham + kāra is the proposed compound analysis; a full grammatical derivation is unchecked.

## Textual evidence

### Sāṃkhya Kārikā 24

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_IzvarakRSNa-sAMkhyakArikA.htm).

```text
abhimāno 'haṃkāras tasmād dvividhaḥ pravartate sargaḥ /
ekādaśakaś ca gaṇas tanmātraḥ pañcakaś caiva //
```

Literal English: Ahaṃkāra is self-appropriation; from it a twofold creation proceeds: the group of eleven and the fivefold group of subtle elements.

## Reading of the proposed English

I-maker is a useful metaphor; I-making better preserves an activity noun. Abhimāna is self-appropriation or self-identification here, not only speaking the words mine and me.
