# apāna

English candidates: **downward wind; elimination**.

## Root and derivation

The prefix/root analysis proposed for the vital winds has **not yet been checked against a grammatical source**. Functional translation is kept separate from derivation.

## Textual evidence

### Suśruta Saṃhitā, Nidānasthāna 1.19

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/susrutasamhita.xml).

```text
pakvādhānālayo+apānaḥ kāle karṣati cāpyadhaḥ/ samīraṇaḥ śakṛnmūtraṃ śukragarbhārtavāni ca//
```

Literal English: Apāna, dwelling in the receptacle of digested material, at the proper time draws down faeces, urine, semen, fetus and menstrual discharge.

### Caraka Saṃhitā, Cikitsāsthāna 28.10–11

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/carakasamhita.xml).

Literal English of the passage: The testes, bladder, penis, navel, thighs, groins and anus are the seats of apāna. Situated in the bowel, it discharges semen, urine and faeces, and menstrual discharge and the fetus. When coordinated and situated in their own seats, these [five winds] perform their own actions; by them the body is sustained free of illness.

Comparison: The discharge functions agree with Suśruta. The close of verse 11 returns to all five winds; it is not a further definition of apāna alone. Release covers reproductive functions here as well as elimination.

## Reading of the proposed English

Release is defensible but broad: the text also covers reproduction, childbirth and menstruation, not merely discarded waste. SARIT represents elided initial vowels by `+`; that editorial notation is retained. Anusvāra glyph ṁ is normalized to ṃ.
