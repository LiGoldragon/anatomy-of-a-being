# citta

English candidates: **mind; thought**.

## Root and derivation

**Checked secondary lexical evidence:** Charles R. Lanman, A Sanskrit Reader, printed vocabulary p. 156 (scan file lan_188.pdf), gives cittá, notice/thought/mind, literally “noticed”, √cit. [Digitized original](https://www.sanskrit-lexicon.uni-koeln.de/scans/LANScan/LANScanpdf/lan_188.pdf). Dhātupāṭha 1.39 reads `citī sañjñāne`, checked in its text. A full Pāṇinian participle derivation remains unchecked.

## Textual evidence

### Pātañjalayogaśāstra, sūtra 1.2 and its Bhāṣya

Status: **checked in the Sanskrit e-text**. This is supplementary Yoga evidence, not a passage of Caraka, Suśruta or the Sāṃkhya Kārikā. [SARIT text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/patanjalayogasastra.xml).

Sūtra:

```text
yogaś cittavṛttinirodhaḥ
```

Literal English: Yoga is the stilling of the activities of citta.

Bhāṣya on 1.2, defining sentence:

```text
cittaṃ hi prakhyāpravṛttisthitiśīlatvāt triguṇam.
```

Literal English: Citta is three-guṇa, because its nature is illumination, activity and stability.

This describes the constitution of the mental instrument; it is not a definition of citta as a passive repository of what has previously been noticed.

## Reading of the proposed English

The participial gloss noticed is lexically defensible, but the technical noun in Yoga is broader than stored impressions. Mind or mental instrument is safer than “the Noticed” or “Memory-ground”. The checked Bhāṣya explicitly describes citta through all three guṇas.
