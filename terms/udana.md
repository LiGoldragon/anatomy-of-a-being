# udāna

English candidates: **upward wind; expression**.

## Root and derivation

The prefix/root analysis proposed for the vital winds has **not yet been checked against a grammatical source**. Functional translation is kept separate from derivation.

## Textual evidence

### Suśruta Saṃhitā, Nidānasthāna 1.14–15

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/susrutasamhita.xml).

```text
prāyaśaḥ kurute duṣṭo hikkāśvāsādikān gadān// udāno nāma yastūrdhvamupaiti pavanottamaḥ//
tena bhāṣitagītādiviśeṣo+abhipravartate/ ūrdhvajatrugatān rogān karoti ca viśeṣataḥ//
```

Literal English: The corrupted [wind] generally makes hiccup, breathing difficulty and similar diseases. The foremost wind which goes upward is called udāna. By it differences of speech, song and so forth proceed; it especially makes diseases above the collarbones.

### Caraka Saṃhitā, Cikitsāsthāna 28.7

Status: **checked against the Sanskrit e-text**, not collated against a manuscript or printed scan. [Text](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/blob/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363/carakasamhita.xml).

Literal English of the passage: The seats of udāna are the navel, chest and throat. Its actions include the setting-in-motion of speech, effort, energy, strength and complexion.

Comparison: Speech agrees with Suśruta’s account; effort, energy, strength and complexion extend beyond communication. Caraka’s list of seats also includes the navel, so the subtype should not be reduced to a head-only organ.

## Reading of the proposed English

Expression is a functional analogy supported by speech and song, but udāna means more than outward communication. Verse 14 starts by completing the prāṇa account. SARIT represents elided initial vowels by `+`; that editorial notation is retained. Anusvāra glyph ṁ is normalized to ṃ.
