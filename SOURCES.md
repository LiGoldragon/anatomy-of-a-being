# Verification method and sources

“Checked” means the cited Sanskrit was read in the named e-text and its local reference was checked. It does not mean comparison with every edition or manuscript. Literal English is newly written here; alternative English labels are proposals. A root’s dictionary meaning alone never proves a noun’s derivation. Traditional nirvacana and historical etymology are different kinds of explanation. Unchecked claims stay explicitly unchecked.

## Local evidence consulted first

The existing caraka-samhita checkout contains curated English digests and Priya Vrat Sharma chapter transcription. Its own README says the digests are not verified Sanskrit. The library has a Vidyāsāgara Sanskrit PDF with noisy OCR, and Suśruta Bhishagratna OCR bundles. The local Sanskrit OCR was inspected but did not supply a reliable searchable reading of the targeted opening verses. It is not the textual witness for the quotations here. No change was made to those repositories.

## Read Sanskrit sources

- [SARIT corpus](https://github.com/sarit/SARIT-corpus/tree/1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363), commit `1eac9ee0b055c8d11147edac4a75c76008ccc363`. Caraka is based on the Yādavaśarman Trivikramātmaja Ācārya edition (1941, reprint 1981), input by Tsutomu Yamashita, Yasutaka Muroya, Yukio Yamanaka and Madhu K. P.; TEI by Dominik Wujastyk. Suśruta is transcribed by Yamashita and Muroya from Ācārya’s 1931/1938 Bombay editions. SARIT distributes both under [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/). Quotations omit XML labels and editorial footnotes; Suśruta’s editorial plus sign for elision is retained and ṁ is rendered ṃ.
- [GRETIL Nirukta](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_yAska-nirukta.htm). References 2.3, 2.16, 3.5 and 3.15 were read for derivations. E-text word-separating dots are replaced by spaces in short quotations.

## Scope of completeness

The initial assignment names a finite set of terms and quotations, not every defining passage in the Sanskrit corpus. Pages collect checked passages relevant to that assignment; none claims an exhaustive list of every definition. The source proposals and private operational context are not republished.

## Further e-texts checked

- [Kaṭha Upaniṣad](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_kathopaniSad.htm), GRETIL, entered by the Vienna University Student Team, based on Patrick Olivelle, The Early Upaniṣads (1998); version 2020-07-31.
- [Sāṃkhya Kārikā](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/corpustei/transformations/html/sa_IzvarakRSNa-sAMkhyakArikA.htm), GRETIL, contribution Ferenc Ruzsa, version 2020-07-31. This witness carries variant readings; quotations use its main reading with editorial segmentation hyphens and apparatus markers removed, not a silent conflation of variants.
- [Bhagavad Gītā, critical-text extraction](https://gretil.sub.uni-goettingen.de/gretil/1_sanskr/2_epic/mbh/ext/bhgce__u.htm), GRETIL, with Mahābhārata concordances. Numbering excludes the introductory Arjuna question.
- [Pāṇinian Dhātupāṭha](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_z_misc_major_works/dhatupatha.html), Sanskrit Documents, non-accented e-text encoded by Mihas Bayaryn. Numbers refer to this edition; alternate Dhātupāṭhas can number entries differently. Only explicitly identified entries were checked, not every possible homonymous root.
- Pāṇini 5.2.93, [Sanskrit Library](https://www.sanskritlibrary.org/grammatical/data/A.5.2.93.html), compared with the GRETIL Aṣṭādhyāyī.
